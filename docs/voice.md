# Voice — working guide

Matt's project writing comes from curiosity, personal use and delight in figuring something out. Keep that grounded in the actual work. A design system cannot manufacture his experiences.

- Lead with what the project is and what the visitor can do or discover.
- Explain why Matt cared when he has supplied that story. Preserve his enthusiasm and specific details; do not replace them with a polished generic persona.
- Keep short notes short. A breadcrumb does not need an article, a slogan or a feature list.
- Remove sentences that add neither meaning, evidence, orientation nor action. Empty space is preferable to synthetic first-person filler.
- Distinguish original projects, contributions, reconstructions, experiments and releases accurately. Never invent personal anecdotes, diary entries, testimonials, usage counts, capabilities or completion status.
- Labels name actions and destinations. Loading/error text tells the truth and gives a recovery path when one exists.

The Tallboy passage about literate programming that paints, the Stone Rose reverse-engineering story and the original MUD enrichment idea carry real reasons to care. Their specificity is the model. Do not copy those phrases into unrelated projects to imitate a brand voice.

Avoid generic technology-marketing claims such as seamless experiences, limitless possibilities and revolutionary tools. Keep detailed setup and developer reference material in focused documents linked from the public introduction. Do not turn a personal project page into an unsolicited contribution programme.
